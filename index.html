What it is

What is this, exactly?

Three levels, like a real company. You talk to an assistant. It passes the big things to a chief executive who plans them. The plan goes to workers who do the job with real tools. The one that plans has no tools of its own.

You talk to

The assistant

Voice or typing. Small things it handles on the spot. Big things go upstairs, and you get told what happened.

Which plans

The chief executive

Turns your ask into a plan, picks who does what, and checks what comes back. It has no way into your email, your calendar, your files or your customers.

Which does the work

The workers

Each one gets only the apps you allow it. You can change which AI it uses, limit what it touches, or pause it.

A comment one of our engineers left in the code, not marketing copy:

“CEO never executes.”

Getting started

Do I have to learn it?

No. You talk to it. Tap the sphere, say what you want in ordinary words, and cut in mid-sentence if it goes the wrong way. There is no interface to master.

  • It is a real conversation, not dictation fed to a chatbot. You can interrupt it.
  • Talking can hand work to the whole team, not just answer a question.
  • It knows the same things whether you speak or type.
  • It can record a meeting you are sitting in and file the transcript into your notes.

Your data

Where do my files live?

On your computer, in a normal folder you can open and back up. It reads and indexes them there, offline. Your documents and your memory never leave the machine, and there is no per-search cost because nothing is being sent anywhere.

It remembers your business

Who your customers are, how you sign off an email, which template you use. It gets better at finding the right thing the more you use it, and it learns how you write from your own emails.

And it stays yours

It is a folder of plain text files. Open one and correct it by hand, and your correction survives. Stop using Workforce 2.0 and the folder stays exactly where it is.

One thing that does leave, and only if you let it

Your own Google key gets used first, always. If your free allowance has been refusing for about 90 seconds and you are signed in, engine work can go through our connection instead so the job finishes rather than dying halfway. Conversations and background housekeeping never do, and you can switch it off. Your files and your memory are not part of this either way.

Staying in control

What stops it doing something I didn't ask for?

You decide how far it can go: read only, draft but never send, or send. Anything that sends, publishes or spends stops and waits for you. If you are not there to answer, it stops rather than sending.

  • Every attempt goes through the same gate. The assistant, a worker, an engine — whoever asked, it is the same check. A new feature cannot forget to ask you.
  • No answer within 90 seconds counts as a no. Not as a quiet yes.
  • Every decision is written down, so you can see what was asked and what you said.
  • It stops at zero. It checks your balance before it starts a job, not after.

The reason, again from the code:

“unattended runs should not silently send messages to real people.”

Questions people ask first

Do I need to be technical to use it?

No. You talk to it or type at it in ordinary words, and it hands the work out. Setup is one free Google key, which takes about two minutes, and the app walks you through it on first launch.

Do I need an API key, and is it free?

Yes, and yes. You need a free Google key, which is a password that lets the app use Google's AI on your behalf. You create it at aistudio.google.com/apikey with any Google account. There is no card to enter and no charge. The steps, with screenshots →

Will I get a surprise bill?

No. Every job is either free, because it ran on your own free Google key, or a fixed price shown before it starts. Nothing is billed per token, and it stops at zero rather than running a balance you have to settle later. Three worked months →

What happens to what we have learned if someone leaves?

It stays. Everyone in the company works into one shared memory that syncs hourly in both directions, so notes, decisions, reference material and the conversations behind them belong to the business rather than to one person's laptop. The next person can search it and ask about it.

Not yet. Workforce 2.0 runs on Windows, and there is an Android build. A Mac version is not available today.
