IPO analysis

It reads the offer document. Each IPO gets one number, with the working shown.

For Indian mainboard and SME IPOs: the chance each one closes above its issue price on listing day, worked out from exchange data, the prospectus and the news, and tested against IPOs that have already listed.

Informational analysis, not investment advice. Past IPO patterns do not guarantee results.

Which IPOs does it cover, and where does the data come from?

NSE and BSE mainboard, plus NSE Emerge and BSE SME. Issue data comes from the exchanges, and the offer document is the red herring prospectus filed with SEBI.

  • From NSE: dates, price band, lot size, issue size, lead managers, and live subscription by category (QIB, NII, retail, employee).
  • From BSE: the issues listed only there, which is where most SME IPOs go.
  • Listing-day prices come from the exchanges' official daily bhavcopy, the only source that covers SME listings.
  • It can also follow US IPOs on Nasdaq and NYSE, from SEC filings.

How does it read the prospectus?

With code, not AI. It finds the financial statements, the peer comparison and the KPI tables, and computes the ratios: valuation against peers, return on capital, revenue growth, debt, and how much of the issue is existing holders selling.

  • A figure that fails the accounting identities a correct read must satisfy is dropped and shown as missing, not shown wrong.
  • Red and green flags follow fixed rules, such as a P/E more than 1.5 times the peer median, or more than 70% of the issue sold by existing holders.
  • From the anchor allocation letter, a row is kept only when shares times price equals the amount.

What are the formulas?

Five. Four perspectives, each its own formula over its own inputs, and a final one that combines them into the IPO's probability.

FormulaWhat goes into it
FundamentalsP/E against peers, return on capital, revenue growth, debt to equity
Institutional investorsQIB and overall subscription, share sold by existing holders, the anchors' past IPO record
Company sentimentNews about the company, from the prospectus date to listing
Macro effectsSector news, market momentum, how recent IPOs listed, SME board
Final probabilityThe four perspectives together

A missing input counts as neutral rather than guessed, so an IPO with no subscription data yet is neither helped nor hurt by the gap.

How do I know the score means anything?

Run the backtest. It replays past NSE IPOs with only what was known by their last bid day, and news searches are date-bounded so no later article can leak in.

  1. Score five

    The oldest untested batch, with the current formulas, which have not seen them.

  2. Check against listing day

    Each formula is marked right or wrong against the real close.

  3. Refit and rescore

    All five formulas are refit on every IPO tested so far.

  4. Stop when settled

    After three batches in a row where nothing moved and nothing improved, with at least 20 tested.

From the backtest screen:

“The first call is the honest measure: the formula had not seen that IPO.”

What does the verdict look like?

One number: the chance the stock closes above its issue price on listing day, with the main reasons for and against and the four perspective scores beside it. There is no buy or avoid label.

  • Until 100 past IPOs have been tested, it calls the number an uncalibrated model score and tells you to read it as a ranking against other IPOs, not a measured chance.
  • Before the price band is announced, it does not score at all.
  • After listing, each IPO is marked called it or missed.

How does it stay current?

A daily refresh finds new issues, reads their documents, updates subscription, rescores, and records the listing-day close of IPOs that have listed. A Refresh button runs it whenever you want.

  • Follow an IPO and you get an alert in the app when its probability moves by 10 points or more.
  • The daily digest is opt-in and off by default. Turn it on and it emails the IPOs closing in the next three days, once a day, only when there are some.
  • The Engine on/off switch pauses the daily refresh. Buttons you press inside the engine still work.

What does it cost?

Each IPO analysed is 1 task, and so is each data job the engine runs (a refresh, results, history). Turn the engine off and the daily refresh stops using tasks.

Questions

Is this investment advice?

No. It is informational analysis. Past IPO patterns do not guarantee results, and it never places an order.

Does it use the grey market premium?

No. GMP is not one of its inputs, and news that reports a GMP figure is given no weight.

Where does AI come in?

In three places only: a summary of each IPO's news, reading an anchor letter that is a scan with no text, and an optional second opinion from an expert you have taught. A news point whose quote is not in the cited article is dropped. The numbers, ratios and formulas are code.

Does it run on Android?

No. IPO analysis runs on the desktop app.
