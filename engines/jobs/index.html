How does it know a job is still open?

It opens every link the way a person would and runs ten checks before showing it to you. Anything that fails is dropped. This is the part job boards do not do, and it is why their listings waste your evenings.

  • The site is gone entirely.
  • The page returns an error.
  • The posting expired, but the page still loads fine and quietly sends you to a jobs index.
  • It is a help page.
  • It is a sign-in page.
  • It is the company's whole careers page, not one job.
  • The page says it is no longer accepting applications.
  • There is no job description on it at all.
  • It is a list of jobs. "Remote Product Manager Jobs" is a list, not a job.
  • It redirects somewhere unrelated.

The third one is the one that matters most. A posting that was filled in March often still returns a perfectly healthy page, which is why you keep applying into nothing.

What does "roles you can actually get" mean?

Matching is weighted toward what your CV can realistically win, not toward what you would enjoy. A list of forty jobs you will not hear back from is worse than six you might.

Will it invent experience I don't have?

No. Every claim it writes is checked back against the text of your actual CV. If it is not in there, it does not go in the letter.

  • A phrase blocklist strips the filler adverbs that make a letter read as machine-written.
  • It rewrites how your real experience is presented for each job. It does not add experience.

This matters more than it sounds. A letter that overstates you is worse than no letter, because you have to sit in the interview and account for it.

Can I change what it wrote?

Yes, in place. The PDF rebuilds from your version, not from the original draft, so what you changed is what gets sent.

What you get

A PDF. Word documents go in as your source CV; they do not come out. If an employer insists on .docx, this will not produce one.

Questions

Does it apply for me automatically?

It writes the documents. You send them. Anything that leaves the building needs your approval, and a job application is exactly the sort of thing you should read first.

Where does it find the jobs?

It searches, then verifies each one it finds. The verification is the valuable half.

Is my CV uploaded anywhere?

Can it write in another language?
