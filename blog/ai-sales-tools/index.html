Look beyond a better opening line

Published 30 September 2026 by the Workforce team. Writing assistance is useful, but an outbound process also needs suitable prospects, contact checks, sending and follow-up. A workflow brings those steps together so you can inspect where work is waiting.

Start with a clear customer profile

Describe the customers you serve and the places you want to search. Workforce uses a browser to find companies, checks fit and contact domains, and filters generic addresses. Review the result before accepting leads. Finding is free; accepting one uses a task.

Make the draft yours

Give the engine useful context and edit its suggested outreach. It learns from what you actually send, rather than treating all of its own generated messages as good examples. A draft should explain a relevant reason to talk, not just insert a prospect’s name into a template.

Approve a manageable outreach run

Start with a small batch you can review properly. Sending is locked by default; you can approve messages individually or as a batch, and can change the engine’s permission setting. Each generated draft and sent message uses one task. A follow-up is another sent message.

Do not automate away reply handling

Workforce does not detect sales replies or automatically process opt-out requests. Read replies yourself and update the shared do-not-contact list. That list is checked at send time so a recorded opt-out can stop a queued message. This is an essential part of running the workflow responsibly.

Keep data claims precise

Prospect records and memory are stored locally, and team sharing can sync through your own Drive. AI requests use online services. Local storage does not mean no information is ever transmitted, and retrieval or templates cannot eliminate every AI error.

Judge the process by useful work

Compare the time spent finding suitable prospects, correcting drafts and handling follow-up with your previous process. Free includes 300 tasks monthly; paid plans add capacity and features. No tool can promise closed deals, perfect deliverability or a process that never needs your judgment.
