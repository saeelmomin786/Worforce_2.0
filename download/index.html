Setup

What do I need before I start?

A Windows PC and a free Google key. A key is a password that lets the app use Google's AI on your behalf. You create it in about two minutes, there is no card to enter, and it does not cost anything.

  1. Open the key page

    Go to aistudio.google.com/apikey and sign in with any Google account. A personal Gmail account works.

  2. Click Create API key

    Google shows you a long string of letters and numbers. That is the key. In the app's own words: it's free, no card needed.

  3. Copy it

    Use the copy button next to it. You will not need to remember it or type it out.

  4. Paste it into Workforce

    On first launch the app asks for it. Paste it in and it checks the key works before moving on, so you find out immediately if something went wrong.

What people worry about, answered

Will Google charge me? No. The free tier does not take a card.

Is there a limit? There is a daily allowance, and everyday use does not reach it. If you do run it out, the app keeps going: signed-in users have their engine work carried on a fallback connection so a job finishes rather than dying halfway through.

Where does the key live? On your machine. It never reaches a web page, and it is not in the download or in our code. More on that →

Day one

What happens in the first ten minutes?

You paste the key, connect the apps you want it to use, and give it one thing to do. You watch it get handed out and come back. Nothing sends anywhere until you say so.

  1. It opens on the console

    One box to type in, and a sphere to tap if you would rather talk. That is the whole interface to begin with.

  2. Connect what you want it to reach

    Gmail, Drive, Calendar and the rest take a sign-in. The ones that need no account are already working. You can skip this and do it later.

  3. Set how far it can go

    Read only, draft but never send, or send. Most people start on draft-only for the first week, which is the sensible way to do it.

  4. Ask for one thing

    Say it out loud or type it. The assistant either handles it or passes it up, and you watch the work appear as tasks rather than as a spinner.

Three good first asks

“Find me twenty companies near Pune that fit the customers I already have.”

“Read my inbox from this week and tell me what I have been asked to do.”

“What are my three closest competitors charging?”

How does it update?

By itself, when you say yes. It checks at launch and puts an Update button in the menu when there is something new. Updates carry code only, so nothing you have made or connected is touched.

It improves itself. Is that safe?

The version you are running is never touched until you approve a change. It works on a copy, has the result checked, and shows it to you. You promote it or you bin it.

  1. It saves what works

    The current version is pinned as the one to fall back to.

  2. It builds a candidate somewhere separate

    Not in the version you are using.

  3. The candidate gets checked

    It has to pass before you are shown it at all.

  4. You decide

    Promote it or discard it. Nothing changes on its own.

And if a new version turns out to be broken

If a promoted version fails to start twice, it puts the old one back by itself. You can also restore any earlier version whenever you want. Crashes are written to a log on your machine so there is something to look at rather than a shrug.

What can it read out of the box?

Word, Excel, PowerPoint, PDF and CSV files go in. PDFs come out, and it can read and update spreadsheets. This is what the tailored CVs and the dashboards are standing on.
